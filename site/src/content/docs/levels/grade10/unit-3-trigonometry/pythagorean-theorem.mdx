> **Question:** How do I find a missing side in a right triangle?

## Think of three squares

The square built on the longest side has exactly the same area as the two smaller squares added together.

<TriangleVisual mode="pythagoras" />

## The one formula

$$
a^2+b^2=c^2
$$

- `a` and `b` are the **legs** that form the right angle.
- `c` is the **hypotenuse**, opposite the right angle.
- The hypotenuse is always the longest side.

## Worked example — find the hypotenuse

**A right triangle has legs of 6 cm and 8 cm. Find the hypotenuse.**

1. Mark the side opposite `90°` as `c`.
2. Write the formula: $a^2+b^2=c^2$.
3. Substitute: $6^2+8^2=c^2$.
4. Simplify: $36+64=c^2$, so $100=c^2$.
5. Take the square root: $c=10$.

**Answer:** The hypotenuse is **10 cm**. It is longer than both legs, so the answer makes sense.

## Add or subtract?

| Missing side | Setup | Memory hook |
|---|---|---|
| Hypotenuse `c` | $c=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}$ | Finding the whole: add |
| Leg `a` | $a=\sqrt{c^2-b^2}$ | Finding a part: subtract |
| Leg `b` | $b=\sqrt{c^2-a^2}$ | Finding a part: subtract |

## Worked example — find a leg

**A ladder is 13 m long. Its foot is 5 m from a wall. How high does it reach?**

The ladder is the hypotenuse because it is opposite the right angle.

$$
5^2+h^2=13^2
$$

$$
25+h^2=169
$$

$$
h^2=144 \quad\Rightarrow\quad h=12
$$

**Answer:** The ladder reaches **12 m** up the wall.

## Formal meaning

In any right triangle, the sum of the squares of the legs equals the square of the hypotenuse.

## When you see… think…

| When you see… | Think… |
|---|---|
| A marked or stated right angle | Pythagoras may be possible |
| Two known sides and one missing side | Use $a^2+b^2=c^2$ |
| Need the hypotenuse | Add the squared legs |
| Need a leg | Subtract the other squared leg from $c^2$ |
| Need an angle | Pythagoras will not find it; use SOH CAH TOA |

## Reasonableness checks

- A length must be positive.
- The hypotenuse must be the longest side.
- If finding a leg, it must be shorter than the hypotenuse.
- The theorem cannot be used unless the triangle is right-angled.

## Common exam mistakes

- Calling a leg `c` instead of marking the side opposite `90°`.
- Adding when a leg is missing.
- Forgetting to square a known side.
- Forgetting the final square root.
- Rounding before the final step.
- Using the theorem on a non-right triangle.

## Quick check

1. A right triangle has legs 9 cm and 12 cm. Find the hypotenuse.
2. A right triangle has hypotenuse 17 cm and one leg 8 cm. Find the other leg.
3. Can you use Pythagoras when all three angles are acute?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

**1. Hypotenuse**

$$c=\sqrt{9^2+12^2}=\sqrt{81+144}=\sqrt{225}=15$$

**Answer:** 15 cm.

**2. Missing leg**

$$b=\sqrt{17^2-8^2}=\sqrt{289-64}=\sqrt{225}=15$$

**Answer:** 15 cm.

**3. Right-angle check**

No. Pythagoras requires a right triangle, and a triangle with three acute angles has no `90°` angle.

</details>
