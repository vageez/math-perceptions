> **Question:** How do I solve a non-right triangle using a known opposite pair?

## Think of matching partners

Every angle is partnered with the side directly across from it. Never separate the partners.

<TriangleVisual mode="sine" />

## The formula

For a missing side:

$$
\frac{a}{\sin A}=\frac{b}{\sin B}=\frac{c}{\sin C}
$$

For a missing angle, flipping every ratio can make the algebra easier:

$$
\frac{\sin A}{a}=\frac{\sin B}{b}=\frac{\sin C}{c}
$$

Lowercase side `a` is opposite uppercase angle `A`; the same pairing holds for `b/B` and `c/C`.

## The recognition test

Before using the Sine Law, circle:

1. one **complete pair**: a known side and its known opposite angle;
2. the **target pair**: the side or angle you need and its partner.

If there is no complete pair, the Sine Law cannot start yet.

## Worked example — find a side

**In triangle ABC, $A=42°$, $a=9$ cm, and $B=71°$. Find side $b$.**

1. The complete pair is `a = 9` opposite `A = 42°`.
2. The target is `b` opposite `B = 71°`.
3. Set up matching pairs:

$$
\frac{b}{\sin71°}=\frac{9}{\sin42°}
$$

4. Multiply:

$$
b=\frac{9\sin71°}{\sin42°}\approx12.72
$$

**Answer:** $b\approx12.7$ cm. Since `71° > 42°`, side `b` should be longer than side `a`; it is.

## Worked example — find an angle

**Side $a=8$ cm is opposite $A=35°$. Side $b=11$ cm is opposite $B$. Find $B$.**

$$
\frac{\sin B}{11}=\frac{\sin35°}{8}
$$

$$
\sin B=\frac{11\sin35°}{8}\approx0.7887
$$

$$
B=\sin^{-1}(0.7887)\approx52.1°
$$

**Answer:** $B\approx52.1°$. The longer side `11` faces the larger angle, so the size is reasonable.

## The triangle check

- Angles total `180°`.
- A larger side must face a larger angle.
- Every angle must be between `0°` and `180°`.

### A caution about SSA

When two sides and a non-included angle are given, the same sine value can sometimes produce a second possible angle: $180°-B$. Use the diagram and problem conditions to decide whether a second triangle is possible. Follow your teacher’s expectations for the **ambiguous case**.

## Multi-step pattern

Sometimes you must first:

- find the third angle using $A+B+C=180°$;
- use SOH CAH TOA in a right-triangle section;
- find a missing length before a complete opposite pair exists.

Do one step at a time and keep unrounded values.

## When you see… think…

| When you see… | Think… |
|---|---|
| A side and its opposite angle | Complete pair |
| Non-right triangle + complete pair | Sine Law |
| Need a side | Put sides on top |
| Need an angle | Put sine expressions on top; use inverse sine |
| Two known angles | Find the third with `180°` if needed |

## Common exam mistakes

- Pairing a side with a nearby angle instead of its opposite angle.
- Starting without a complete known pair.
- Using regular sine instead of inverse sine for an angle.
- Mixing degrees and radians.
- Rounding an intermediate ratio.
- Ignoring the larger-side/larger-angle check.

## Quick check

1. Which side is paired with angle `C`?
2. Can the Sine Law start if you know only sides `a`, `b`, and `c`?
3. If `a = 5`, `A = 30°`, and `B = 80°`, find `b`.

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Side `c`, directly opposite angle `C`.
2. Not directly: there is no known angle and therefore no complete side–angle pair. If area is required, use Heron’s Formula.
3. $b=\dfrac{5\sin80°}{\sin30°}\approx9.85$.

</details>
