> **Question:** How do I find triangle area when I know all three sides but not the height?

## Think of area without dropping a height

Heron’s Formula turns the three side lengths directly into area. The bridge is the **semiperimeter**—half the distance around the triangle.

<TriangleVisual mode="heron" />

## The two-step formula

First find the semiperimeter:

$$
s=\frac{a+b+c}{2}
$$

Then find the area:

$$
A=\sqrt{s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)}
$$

`a`, `b`, and `c` are the three side lengths. `s` is the semiperimeter. `A` is area.

## Worked example

**Find the area of a triangle with side lengths 5 cm, 5 cm, and 6 cm.**

1. Find the semiperimeter:

$$
s=\frac{5+5+6}{2}=8
$$

2. Substitute every factor:

$$
A=\sqrt{8(8-5)(8-5)(8-6)}
$$

3. Simplify inside the root:

$$
A=\sqrt{8(3)(3)(2)}=\sqrt{144}=12
$$

**Answer:** The area is **12 cm²**.

## Why “semi” matters

The perimeter is `a + b + c`. Heron uses **half** of it.

```text
perimeter = a + b + c
semiperimeter = perimeter ÷ 2
```

Using the full perimeter changes every factor and gives the wrong area.

## Can the sides form a triangle?

Before calculating, check the **triangle inequality**:

> Any two sides added together must be longer than the third side.

For `2, 3, 6`, the check fails because `2 + 3` is not greater than `6`. Those lengths cannot close into a triangle.

## Calculator entry

Enter the whole product under one square root:

`√(s × (s−a) × (s−b) × (s−c))`

Use brackets around each subtraction and round only the final answer.

## Formal meaning

Heron’s Formula gives the area of any triangle when all three side lengths are known. No height or angle is required.

## When you see… think…

| When you see… | Think… |
|---|---|
| Three sides and need area | Heron’s Formula |
| Letter `s` | Semiperimeter, not full perimeter |
| No height | That is fine; Heron does not need it |
| Two sides plus included angle | Use the SAS area formula instead |

## Common exam mistakes

- Calling the formula “Hero’s Formula”; the standard name is **Heron’s Formula**.
- Using the full perimeter instead of the semiperimeter.
- Forgetting one of the four factors.
- Taking the square root of only one factor.
- Entering subtraction without brackets.
- Reporting centimetres instead of square centimetres.

## Quick check

1. Find `s` for side lengths 7, 8, and 9.
2. Which units should a triangle area use if the sides are metres?
3. Can side lengths 4, 5, and 10 form a triangle?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $s=(7+8+9)/2=12$.
2. Square metres, `m²`.
3. No. `4 + 5 = 9`, which is not greater than `10`.

</details>
