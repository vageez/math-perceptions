> **Question:** How do I find area from two sides and the angle between them?

## Think of the angle being pinched

The two known sides meet and **pinch** the included angle. The angle in the formula must be the one between those two sides.

<TriangleVisual mode="sas" />

## The formula

$$
A=\frac12 ab\sin C
$$

Sides `a` and `b` form angle `C`. Equivalent versions are:

$$
A=\frac12 bc\sin A
\qquad
A=\frac12 ca\sin B
$$

Each angle must be paired with the two sides that form it.

## Worked example

**Two sides of a triangle are 8 cm and 11 cm. The angle between them is $47°$. Find the area.**

1. The known angle is between the two known sides, so this is SAS area.
2. Write the formula: $A=\tfrac12 ab\sin C$.
3. Substitute: $A=\tfrac12(8)(11)\sin47°$.
4. Calculate in degree mode: $A\approx32.18$.

**Answer:** The area is approximately **32.2 cm²**.

## Why the included angle matters

The formula is another way to write:

$$
A=\frac12(\text{base})(\text{height})
$$

If side `a` is the base, the vertical height created by side `b` is `b sin C`. Therefore:

$$
A=\frac12(a)(b\sin C)
$$

That is why `C` must sit between sides `a` and `b`.

## A useful check at 90°

Because $\sin90°=1$:

$$
A=\frac12 ab\sin90°=\frac12 ab
$$

So the formula becomes the familiar area of a right triangle.

## Choose the correct pair

| Known angle | Use the two sides that meet there | Formula |
|---|---|---|
| `A` | `b` and `c` | $A=\tfrac12bc\sin A$ |
| `B` | `c` and `a` | $A=\tfrac12ca\sin B$ |
| `C` | `a` and `b` | $A=\tfrac12ab\sin C$ |

## When you see… think…

| When you see… | Think… |
|---|---|
| Two sides + angle between them + area | SAS area formula |
| Three sides + area | Heron’s Formula |
| A non-included angle | Do not use it with those two sides |
| A `90°` included angle | The formula reduces to `½ × base × height` |

## Common exam mistakes

- Using an angle that is not between the two chosen sides.
- Forgetting the factor `1/2`.
- Multiplying by the angle instead of `sin(angle)`.
- Leaving the calculator in radian mode.
- Mixing length units.
- Reporting linear units instead of square units.

## Quick check

1. Which angle is included between sides `a` and `b`?
2. Find the area when `a = 6`, `b = 9`, and `C = 30°`.
3. If all three sides are known but no angle is known, which area method fits?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Angle `C`.
2. $A=\tfrac12(6)(9)\sin30°=13.5$ square units.
3. Heron’s Formula.

</details>
