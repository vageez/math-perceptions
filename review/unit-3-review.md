# Grade 10 Visualization Study Guide
## Unit 3 — Trigonometry: Design and Review Specification

**Document purpose:** Expert review handoff

**Deliverable:** A self-contained visual study booklet for Unit 3

**Audience:** Grade 10 students preparing for classroom assessments and final exams

**Source scope:** [Grade10Math.com — Unit 3: Trigonometry](https://www.grade10math.com/unit-3)

---

## 1. Project Objective

Create and refine a concise, accurate, and memorable visualization study guide for **Unit 3: Trigonometry**.

This guide is not intended to read like a traditional textbook. It should function as a collection of visual reference pages that help students answer four questions quickly:

1. **What information does the triangle give me?**
2. **What am I trying to find?**
3. **Which formula or method fits those clues?**
4. **How do I use it correctly?**

The guide must make formula selection visible. A student should learn to distinguish right-triangle problems from non-right-triangle problems, identify known and unknown sides or angles, and choose an efficient method without being told its name.

> **Primary test:** If a student had only this booklet before the exam, could they recognize the triangle type, choose the correct method, and solve representative Unit 3 problems accurately?

## 2. Source-Aligned Scope

Grade10Math.com organizes Unit 3 into five sections:

1. Pythagoras
2. SOH CAH TOA
3. Sine Law
4. “Hero’s Formula”
5. “Trig Formula”

For accuracy and professional presentation, this specification uses the standard mathematical names:

- **Heron’s Formula** instead of “Hero’s Formula”
- **SAS Triangle Area Formula** for the source section called “Trig Formula”

The linked practice materials indicate the following assessed skills:

- Pythagorean calculations in right triangles;
- SOH CAH TOA for missing sides, missing angles, and multi-step problems;
- Sine Law for missing sides, missing angles, and multi-step problems combined with right-triangle trigonometry;
- Heron’s Formula for the area of a triangle from three side lengths;
- the SAS area formula for the area of a triangle from two sides and the included angle.

The guide should remain faithful to this scope. It may include prerequisite ideas—such as triangle notation, inverse trigonometric functions, and the triangle angle sum—when they are necessary to use the five methods correctly.

## 3. Design Philosophy

The guide should follow these principles:

1. **Visualization before explanation** — Use a labelled triangle or application diagram as the main teaching device.
2. **Method selection before calculation** — Show students how the given information determines the method.
3. **Understanding before memorization** — Connect formulas to sides, angles, and geometric meaning.
4. **Plain language with precise mathematics** — Begin with an intuitive description, then use correct terminology.
5. **Low cognitive load** — Remove unnecessary prose, decorative clutter, and advanced edge cases that are outside the course.
6. **One central question per poster** — Every page element should support that question.
7. **Labels connected to the triangle** — Vocabulary and formula symbols should point directly to corresponding sides or angles.
8. **Consistent visual language** — Reuse colours, notation, callouts, and diagram conventions throughout the unit.
9. **Exam usefulness** — Prioritize recognition, setup, calculator use, units, rounding, and interpretation.
10. **No memorized diagram orientation** — Rotate and reflect triangles so students learn relationships rather than positions.

### Visual Direction

- Use one consistent Unit 3 colour family with accessible contrast.
- Make the triangle or real-world application diagram the dominant element.
- Mark right angles with a square, not only with text.
- Use matching colours or symbols for each opposite side–angle pair.
- Distinguish known values, unknown values, and calculated values visually.
- Use arrows and callouts to show why a formula fits.
- Keep formulas large and easy to locate.
- Do not rely on colour alone; pair colour with labels, line styles, or symbols.
- Ensure mathematical notation remains legible when printed or viewed at reduced size.

### Content Direction

- Use short sentences and familiar words.
- Pair formal terms with plain-English explanations.
- Use original examples rather than reproducing source questions.
- Show enough algebraic steps for a Grade 10 student to follow.
- State calculator mode and rounding expectations consistently.
- Keep side–angle naming conventions consistent across every page.
- Include common diagram variants so students do not depend on a single orientation.
- Separate exact setup from rounded decimal answers.
- Include units in contextual answers.

## 4. Audience and Prerequisites

The intended reader is a Grade 10 student who may:

- find dense mathematical language difficult;
- confuse opposite and adjacent sides;
- have difficulty selecting among several formulas;
- need explicit calculator guidance;
- benefit from diagrams and real-world mental models;
- be reviewing under time pressure.

### Assumed Prerequisite Knowledge

Students should already be able to:

- identify a triangle and a right angle;
- identify and measure angles in degrees;
- use the fact that angles in a triangle total `180°`;
- square numbers and find square roots;
- substitute values into a formula;
- rearrange a simple equation;
- use brackets correctly on a calculator;
- round a decimal to a requested place.

Where one of these skills is essential to a worked method, include a short visual reminder rather than assuming the student will infer the missing step.

## 6. Standard Poster Template

Each poster should answer one central question and use the structure below. Sections may move to support the design, but their instructional purpose should remain consistent.

### A. Title and Guiding Question

Use a direct title and one student-centred question.

Example:

> **SOH CAH TOA — Which ratio connects my known side, unknown side, and reference angle?**

### B. Hero Diagram

The largest element on the page should:

- communicate most of the concept visually;
- show all required labels directly on the triangle;
- link formula symbols to geometric parts;
- make the known information and target unknown obvious;
- provide a visual anchor a student can recall during a test.

### C. Think Of…

Include a short mental model where it improves understanding.

Examples:

- Pythagorean Theorem → three squares attached to a right triangle
- SOH CAH TOA → choosing a tool based on the two sides involved
- Sine Law → matching each side with the angle directly opposite it
- Heron’s Formula → finding area when the triangle gives three sides but no height
- SAS area → two sides “pinching” the included angle

The analogy must clarify the mathematics without replacing the precise rule.

### D. Given–Find–Choose Strip

Every poster should contain a compact recognition strip:

1. **Given:** What information is known?
2. **Find:** What quantity is unknown?
3. **Choose:** Which formula fits those clues?

This strip should use the same visual style throughout the unit.

### E. Key Rules

Include approximately **5–8 concise rules**, or fewer when the method requires fewer. Rules should be actionable, accurate, and relevant to common Grade 10 problems.

### F. Formula Box

Include:

- the formula;
- the meaning of every symbol;
- a diagram using the same symbols;
- when to use it;
- when not to use it;
- any important restriction;
- one brief substitution example where useful.

### G. Calculator Cue

Where applicable, show:

- degree mode;
- the required calculator operation;
- inverse sine, cosine, or tangent notation;
- brackets needed for a correct entry;
- rounding only at the final step.

The guide should distinguish an inverse trigonometric function, such as `sin⁻¹`, from the reciprocal `1/sin`.

### H. Vocabulary

Include only essential terms. Each definition should be no longer than one sentence and should point to the corresponding part of the hero diagram whenever possible.

### I. Recognition Guide

Answer: **“What clues tell me to use this method?”**

Use short prompts such as:

- Right triangle + two sides? → Pythagorean Theorem may fit.
- Right triangle + a non-right angle? → SOH CAH TOA.
- Non-right triangle + a known opposite pair? → Sine Law.
- Three sides and need area? → Heron’s Formula.
- Two sides and the included angle and need area? → SAS area formula.

### J. Worked Example

Include one or two original examples that:

- demonstrate the core method;
- show all necessary steps without becoming text-heavy;
- connect the calculation to the labelled diagram;
- preserve unrounded values until the final step;
- end with a clearly identified answer, units, and interpretation.

### K. Common Mistakes

Include approximately **3–5 high-frequency errors**. Pair each mistake with a brief correction or prevention strategy.

### L. Quick Review

End with a 30-second self-check, such as:

- [ ] I can recognize when this method applies.
- [ ] I can label the triangle correctly.
- [ ] I can choose and write the formula.
- [ ] I can enter the calculation correctly.
- [ ] I can round and label the answer.

## 7. Poster Specifications

### Poster 1 — Pythagorean Theorem

**Guiding question:** How do I find a missing side in a right triangle when two sides are known?

#### Required Hero Diagram

Create one clearly labelled right triangle showing:

- the right-angle marker;
- the two legs `a` and `b`;
- the hypotenuse `c` opposite the right angle;
- a square built on each side;
- the visual relationship “area of square on `a` + area of square on `b` = area of square on `c`.”

Include small secondary diagrams for finding:

- the hypotenuse;
- a missing leg.

#### Required Formula

`a² + b² = c²`

Define:

- `a` and `b` — the legs that form the right angle;
- `c` — the hypotenuse, opposite the right angle and always the longest side.

Show the rearranged forms visually:

- hypotenuse: `c = √(a² + b²)`
- missing leg: `a = √(c² - b²)` or `b = √(c² - a²)`

#### Required Concepts and Rules

- The theorem applies only to right triangles.
- The hypotenuse is always opposite the `90°` angle.
- The hypotenuse must be represented by `c` in the standard formula.
- Add the squared legs to find the squared hypotenuse.
- Subtract a squared leg from the squared hypotenuse to find the other squared leg.
- Take the square root only after isolating the missing side squared.
- The answer should be positive because it represents a length.
- The calculated hypotenuse should be longer than either leg.

#### Essential Vocabulary

- right triangle
- right angle
- leg
- hypotenuse
- square
- square root
- Pythagorean Theorem

#### Recognition Guide

Use the Pythagorean Theorem when:

- the triangle is right-angled;
- two side lengths are known;
- one side length is unknown;
- no angle calculation is required.

Do not use it merely because a diagram is triangular. A visible or stated right angle is required.

#### Required Worked Examples

Include:

1. finding the hypotenuse from two legs;
2. finding a leg from the hypotenuse and the other leg.

At least one example should require rounding, and the other may use a Pythagorean triple for a clean visual check.

#### Common Mistakes to Address

- using the theorem on a non-right triangle;
- assigning `c` to a leg rather than the hypotenuse;
- adding when finding a missing leg instead of subtracting;
- forgetting to square one of the known sides;
- forgetting the final square root;
- rounding before the final step;
- accepting a hypotenuse shorter than a leg.

### Poster 2 — SOH CAH TOA

**Guiding question:** Which trigonometric ratio connects my reference angle, known side, and unknown side?

#### Required Hero Diagram

Create a right triangle with:

- a right-angle marker;
- a clearly marked reference angle `θ`;
- the hypotenuse labelled opposite the right angle;
- the opposite side labelled across from `θ`;
- the adjacent side labelled beside `θ` but not on the hypotenuse;
- colour or symbol matching between each side and the relevant part of SOH CAH TOA.

Include a second version with the reference angle moved so students can see that **opposite** and **adjacent** depend on the chosen angle, while the hypotenuse does not.

#### Required Formulas

`sin θ = opposite / hypotenuse`

`cos θ = adjacent / hypotenuse`

`tan θ = opposite / adjacent`

The mnemonic may be shown as:

- **SOH** — Sine = Opposite ÷ Hypotenuse
- **CAH** — Cosine = Adjacent ÷ Hypotenuse
- **TOA** — Tangent = Opposite ÷ Adjacent

#### Required Concepts and Rules

- SOH CAH TOA applies to right triangles.
- Choose a non-right reference angle before naming opposite and adjacent.
- The hypotenuse is always opposite the right angle.
- Opposite is across from the reference angle.
- Adjacent touches the reference angle but is not the hypotenuse.
- Select the ratio containing the known side and the required side.
- Use `sin`, `cos`, or `tan` to find a side.
- Use `sin⁻¹`, `cos⁻¹`, or `tan⁻¹` to find an angle from a ratio.
- Use the triangle angle sum or the Pythagorean Theorem when a multi-step problem requires another measurement first.

#### Calculator Requirements

Show students how to:

- confirm degree mode;
- calculate a trig ratio;
- use an inverse trig key to find an angle;
- use brackets around a fraction or ratio;
- keep full calculator precision until the final answer.

Explicitly state that `sin⁻¹` means inverse sine in this context; it does not mean `1/sin`.

#### Essential Vocabulary

- trigonometric ratio
- sine
- cosine
- tangent
- reference angle
- opposite
- adjacent
- hypotenuse
- inverse trigonometric function

#### Recognition Guide

Think **SOH CAH TOA** when:

- the triangle is right-angled;
- a non-right angle is known or required;
- two of the relevant sides are known or involved;
- the problem asks for a side or angle.

#### Required Worked Examples

Include:

1. finding a missing side;
2. finding a missing angle using an inverse trig function;
3. one compact multi-step example or decision path.

Use differently oriented triangles across the examples.

#### Common Mistakes to Address

- choosing opposite and adjacent before choosing the reference angle;
- calling a leg the hypotenuse;
- selecting a ratio that omits the known or unknown side;
- using regular trig instead of inverse trig for an angle;
- using inverse trig when finding a side;
- leaving the calculator in radian mode;
- entering a ratio without necessary brackets;
- rounding intermediate values too early.

### Poster 3 — Sine Law

**Guiding question:** How do I solve a non-right triangle when I know a matching side–opposite-angle pair?

#### Required Hero Diagram

Create a non-right triangle labelled:

- vertices or angles `A`, `B`, and `C`;
- opposite sides `a`, `b`, and `c`;
- three visually matched side–angle pairs: `a ↔ A`, `b ↔ B`, and `c ↔ C`.

The diagram must make the word **opposite** visually unmistakable. Avoid placing a side label so close to the wrong angle that students may pair them incorrectly.

Include a compact “known pair + incomplete pair” visual showing why the Sine Law applies.

#### Required Formula

For finding sides:

`a / sin A = b / sin B = c / sin C`

For finding angles, the reciprocal form may be shown:

`sin A / a = sin B / b = sin C / c`

Explain that the two forms represent the same relationship. Select the orientation that places the unknown conveniently in the numerator.

#### Required Concepts and Rules

- Each side is paired with the angle directly opposite it.
- The Sine Law works in non-right and right triangles, but SOH CAH TOA is usually simpler for a right triangle.
- To solve with the Sine Law, identify at least one complete opposite pair.
- Build an equation using the complete pair and the pair containing the unknown.
- Use inverse sine when solving for an angle.
- Use `A + B + C = 180°` when a third angle is required.
- A longer side lies opposite a larger angle; use this relationship as a reasonableness check.
- Multi-step problems may require SOH CAH TOA, Pythagoras, or the triangle angle sum before the Sine Law can be used.

#### Ambiguous Case Review Note

When two sides and a non-included angle are given, inverse sine can sometimes produce two possible triangles. The reviewer should confirm whether the **SSA ambiguous case** is expected in the intended Grade 10 course:

- If it is required, add a clearly separated visual decision panel and a two-solution example.
- If it is not required, do not overload the main poster; include only a brief caution that a diagram or problem conditions may need checking.

#### Essential Vocabulary

- Sine Law
- opposite pair
- non-right triangle
- included angle
- inverse sine
- ambiguous case

#### Recognition Guide

Think **Sine Law** when:

- the triangle is not necessarily right-angled;
- a side and its opposite angle are both known;
- another side or angle is required;
- the information pattern resembles ASA, AAS, or SSA.

Do not choose the Sine Law merely because a sine value appears possible. First identify a complete opposite pair.

#### Required Worked Examples

Include:

1. finding a missing side;
2. finding a missing angle;
3. one compact multi-step example that combines the Sine Law with the angle sum or right-triangle trigonometry.

#### Common Mistakes to Address

- pairing a side with an adjacent angle instead of its opposite angle;
- writing mismatched ratios;
- using the Sine Law without a complete known pair;
- forgetting inverse sine when finding an angle;
- leaving the calculator in radian mode;
- rounding an intermediate ratio too early;
- failing to check that larger sides face larger angles;
- overlooking a second possible angle when the ambiguous case is in scope.

### Poster 4 — Heron’s Formula

**Guiding question:** How do I find the area of a triangle when all three side lengths are known but no height is given?

#### Naming Note

The source page and linked material use **“Hero’s Formula.”** The standard mathematical name is **Heron’s Formula**, named after Heron of Alexandria. The finished guide should use the correct name while noting the source wording only if necessary for cross-reference.

#### Required Hero Diagram

Create a non-right triangle with side lengths `a`, `b`, and `c`. Show:

- no known height;
- the perimeter being halved to form the semiperimeter `s`;
- the four factors `s`, `(s - a)`, `(s - b)`, and `(s - c)` flowing into the square-root expression;
- the final area represented as the shaded region inside the triangle.

The diagram should make the two-stage process memorable:

1. calculate `s`;
2. substitute into Heron’s Formula.

#### Required Formulas

Semiperimeter:

`s = (a + b + c) / 2`

Area:

`A = √[s(s - a)(s - b)(s - c)]`

Define:

- `a`, `b`, and `c` — the three side lengths;
- `s` — semiperimeter, or half the perimeter;
- `A` — area of the triangle.

#### Required Concepts and Rules

- Heron’s Formula finds triangle area from three side lengths.
- It does not require a height or angle.
- Calculate the semiperimeter before using the area formula.
- Use the same units for all three sides.
- Keep brackets around each subtraction.
- Area is reported in square units.
- Preserve full precision until the final answer.
- The three side lengths must be capable of forming a triangle.

Include a brief triangle-inequality check:

> The sum of any two side lengths must be greater than the third side.

#### Essential Vocabulary

- Heron’s Formula
- semiperimeter
- perimeter
- area
- square units
- triangle inequality

#### Recognition Guide

Use Heron’s Formula when:

- all three side lengths are known;
- the area is required;
- no perpendicular height is given;
- no included angle is needed or available.

#### Required Worked Example

Use an original three-side example and show:

1. the semiperimeter calculation;
2. substitution into all four factors;
3. the square-root calculation;
4. the final area with square units.

Choose values that make the visual layout readable and allow a useful reasonableness check.

#### Common Mistakes to Address

- using the full perimeter instead of the semiperimeter;
- forgetting one factor under the square root;
- mixing up `s - a`, `s - b`, and `s - c`;
- omitting brackets in the calculator entry;
- taking the square root of only one factor;
- reporting linear units instead of square units;
- using side lengths that cannot form a triangle;
- rounding `s` or intermediate products too early.

### Poster 5 — SAS Triangle Area Formula

**Guiding question:** How do I find the area of a triangle when two sides and the angle between them are known?

#### Naming and Mental Model

The source labels this section “Trig Formula,” and its linked material calls the method **SAS/2** or “SAS Pinch Trig Area.” The finished guide should use the standard descriptive name **SAS Triangle Area Formula** and may retain **SAS/2** as a supporting mnemonic.

Think of the two known sides as **pinching** the known angle between them.

#### Required Hero Diagram

Create a triangle showing:

- two known sides `a` and `b`;
- the included angle `C` where those two sides meet;
- the included angle visually “pinched” between the sides;
- the triangle’s shaded area;
- a contrasting non-included angle to show which angle would not work with those two side labels.

Where space permits, visually connect the formula to the standard area relationship `A = ½(base)(height)` by showing the height as `b sin C` or an equivalent construction.

#### Required Formulas

`A = ½ab sin C`

Equivalent cyclic forms may be shown:

- `A = ½bc sin A`
- `A = ½ca sin B`

Explain that the chosen angle must be the angle **between** the two chosen sides.

#### Required Concepts and Rules

- The formula finds area from two side lengths and their included angle.
- The included angle is where the two known sides meet.
- Pair the angle with the two sides that form it.
- The method works for right and non-right triangles.
- If the included angle is `90°`, the formula becomes the familiar `A = ½(base)(height)` because `sin 90° = 1`.
- Use degree mode when the angle is measured in degrees.
- Area is reported in square units.
- Keep full precision until the final answer.

#### Essential Vocabulary

- SAS
- included angle
- triangle area
- sine
- square units
- base
- height

#### Recognition Guide

Use the SAS area formula when:

- two side lengths are known;
- the angle between those two sides is known;
- the area is required.

Do not use it with an angle that is not between the two selected sides.

#### Required Worked Examples

Include:

1. a non-right triangle using two sides and their included angle;
2. a small visual check showing that a `90°` included angle reduces to `½(base)(height)`.

#### Common Mistakes to Address

- choosing a non-included angle;
- omitting the factor `½`;
- multiplying by the angle instead of `sin(angle)`;
- leaving the calculator in radian mode;
- using inconsistent length units;
- reporting linear units rather than square units;
- rounding the sine value too early.

## 8. Unit Summary and Method-Selection Guide

Create a one-page visual overview that organizes the unit by **triangle type**, **given information**, and **target quantity**.

The summary should not present the five formulas as interchangeable. It should make selection logic obvious.

### Required Decision Structure

Start with:

> **What kind of triangle and information do I have?**

Then show these branches:

#### Right Triangle

- Two sides known; find a side → **Pythagorean Theorem**
- A non-right angle and side information; find a side or angle → **SOH CAH TOA**

#### Any Triangle / Non-Right Triangle

- Known opposite side–angle pair; find another side or angle → **Sine Law**
- Three sides known; find area → **Heron’s Formula**
- Two sides and their included angle known; find area → **SAS area formula**

### Required Visual Reminders

Include:

- a right-angle marker beside the right-triangle methods;
- a visual opposite pair beside the Sine Law;
- `SSS → area` beside Heron’s Formula;
- `SAS → area` beside the SAS area formula;
- a reminder that angles in a triangle total `180°`;
- a reminder to use degree mode;
- a reminder to round only the final answer.

### Multi-Step Problems

Show that some questions require more than one method. Include one compact pathway such as:

`Find a missing right-triangle side → use SOH CAH TOA → use that result in a larger non-right triangle → apply Sine Law`

The summary should avoid introducing new mathematics.

## 12. Reviewer Tasks

The expert reviewer should assess both the mathematics and the learning design.

### A. Mathematical Accuracy

- [ ] Verify every definition, formula, diagram, substitution, calculator step, and answer.
