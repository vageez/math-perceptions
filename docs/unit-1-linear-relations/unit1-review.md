# Unit 1 Expert Review Handoff

## Review objective

Review the first version of **Unit 1 — Linear Relations** for one practical outcome:

> Can a Grade 10 student use these pages to recognize and solve the common Unit 1 exam questions with confidence?

This is a visual study guide, not a textbook. Recommend added detail only when it improves understanding, memory, recognition, or exam performance.

## Student and use case

- Grade 10 student preparing for quizzes, a unit test, and a final exam.
- Mathematical confidence may be low.
- Plain-language explanations and visible problem patterns should come before formal terminology.
- The student should be able to review the unit in roughly 20–30 minutes after learning it once.

## Intended page pattern

Each concept page should include:

1. One memorable main picture or visual model.
2. A layperson’s analogy before the formal meaning.
3. The minimum rules and formulas needed.
4. “When you see… think…” recognition cues.
5. One or more representative worked examples.
6. Common exam mistakes.
7. A short retrieval check and completion checklist.

## Reviewer questions

### A. Mathematical accuracy

- Are every formula, definition, example, answer, and special case correct?
- Is any wording technically misleading even if it sounds simple?
- Are conventions appropriate for the intended Grade 10 course?

### B. Curriculum completeness

- Does this cover the examinable knowledge and skills in Unit 1?
- Is an essential problem type missing?
- Is anything included that distracts from passing the course?

### C. Recognition and decision-making

- Will the student know which method to choose from the wording of a question?
- Are the “When you see… think…” cues correct and memorable?
- Does the guide distinguish easily confused ideas?

### D. Visual learning

- Does each opening diagram communicate the main idea quickly?
- Which diagram needs to be replaced by a proper illustration or SVG?
- Can labels point more directly to the mathematical object?
- Is any page too dense for a visual learner?

### E. Clarity and cognitive load

- Is the language understandable on the first read?
- Can any sentence, table, or section be shortened without losing usefulness?
- Do examples introduce one difficulty at a time?

### F. Exam readiness

- Are the worked examples representative of likely questions?
- Do the quick checks test actual understanding rather than recognition alone?
- If the student completes the exam checklist honestly, are they likely ready?

### G. Consistency and navigation

- Are `m`, `b`, ordered pairs, intercepts, and method names used consistently?
- Do pages progress in a helpful order?
- Are cross-links complete and easy to follow?

## Requested review format

Please return findings in priority order:

| Priority | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Critical | Incorrect or missing content that could cost marks |
| Important | A change likely to improve understanding or exam performance |
| Polish | A useful but non-essential wording or design improvement |

For each finding, provide:

- file and section;
- issue;
- why it matters to the student;
- exact recommended change.

Then provide:

1. a curriculum coverage verdict;
2. an exam-readiness verdict;
3. the three highest-value visual improvements;
4. a recommendation: **approve**, **approve with revisions**, or **revise and review again**.

## Success criteria

The unit is ready when:

- the mathematics is accurate;
- all core Unit 1 skills are covered;
- a student can quickly recognize which method to use;
- the visual hierarchy is clear;
- explanations use less text than a conventional textbook;
- examples and checks prepare the student to earn marks;
- no section exists only to make the guide look comprehensive.
